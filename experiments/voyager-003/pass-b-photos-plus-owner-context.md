# Voyager Experiment 003 — Pass B: Photos + owner context

## Status

Frozen blinded result. The model received the same ten frozen Voyager images plus ordinary owner context. It was not told the later owner correction that the riding POV predates some later changes or that the current bike state remains unfinished.

## Model response (verbatim)

Pass B changes my read of the story in a meaningful way. The images suggested a restoration/recommissioning narrative in Pass A, but the owner's context makes that too dramatic. This is a smaller, more specific story: **a $35 complete old touring bike with unusually intact provenance, bought partly out of curiosity, then deliberately kept mostly original while being changed just enough to make it pleasant to ride.**

That distinction should drive the edit.

The stronger story is the tension between **preservation and use**.

For $35, the owner got a complete Schwinn Voyager, along with its original receipt, brochure and a photograph from the original owner. The initial impulse was conservative: check it over, keep or restore what was there, and ride it.

But the bike was also bought to be experienced rather than merely preserved. The owner specifically wanted to try features of an older touring bike, including its downtube shifting.

Then came a series of modest decisions. The wheel size might change. Then it doesn't, because inexpensive suitable tires make keeping the original wheels practical. The contact points are a harder decision. Eventually comfort wins: different saddle, different bars, using parts already available and keeping the general touring character. Existing touring racks get added.

So the narrative isn't a conventional restoration and it isn't a custom build. It's more like:

**What do you do with a remarkably complete $35 survivor when you actually want to ride it?**

The model then proposed an image sequence that opened with the bike as acquired, revealed the provenance material, treated the workshop as evaluation/tinkering rather than dramatic restoration, elevated the handlebar image as evidence of the comfort-versus-originality decision, and again chose the riding POV as the closing resolution.

It also recognized that **having ten source images does not mean the story needs ten published images**, and recommended leaving some supporting images out.

## Experiment notes

Later owner review exposed a serious remaining error: the riding POV photograph was taken **before some of the changes** and therefore cannot safely function as the payoff to that transformation. The most recent/current bike image also still shows unfinished work, so the evidence pile does not actually contain the clean finished-state ending the model inferred.

Those corrections are intentionally recorded outside the frozen response because they are evaluation findings, not inputs that the blinded model received.

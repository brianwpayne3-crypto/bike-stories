# Experiment 004 — Clarification 002

This file preserves the second owner clarification and the fresh-context model's immediate response before further evaluation.

## Owner answer

> I don't remember exactly. One of them I believe was a trip to Land Between the Lakes in Kentucky. One of them I believe was the C&O and Gap Trail, and one of them might have been something else. One of them might actually have been preparing for the C&O and Gap Trail. I'm not 100% sure.

## Fresh-context model response

And that uncertainty is itself useful source information. I would **preserve it rather than trying to resolve it artificially**.

At this point I'd record the collection this way:

- **Established:** These are the same bicycle in different configurations over time. Several of the loaded-bike photos relate to preparation for different bikepacking trips.
- **Owner recollection, reasonably confident:** At least some of this material may relate to a **Land Between the Lakes, Kentucky** trip and a **C&O Canal Towpath / Great Allegheny Passage** trip.
- **Owner recollection, uncertain:** One of the images may specifically show **preparation for the C&O/GAP trip rather than the trip itself**, and there may be another trip represented that the owner can't currently identify.
- **Unknown:** Which individual photograph corresponds to which trip. We shouldn't assign them based on appearance.

I also wouldn't keep questioning you until you produce an answer. **"I don't remember" is a valid result.** BikeStories should be capable of preserving a memory with fuzzy boundaries: *these photos are from around the period when I was doing several bikepacking trips; I think these included LBL and C&O/GAP, but I no longer know exactly which setup was for which.*

That's arguably better memory preservation than silently turning uncertain recollections into false metadata.

It also gives us another useful design finding from this experiment: **story identification needs confidence/provenance.** There is a real difference between *visible in the photograph*, *owner says this happened*, *owner thinks this is probably what the photograph represents*, and *unknown*. The system should retain those distinctions rather than flattening everything into "facts."
